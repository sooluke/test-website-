Why Simple Ideas Are Often the Hardest to Build

Simple products often look obvious after they are finished. Getting there, however, can require removing dozens of unnecessary ideas and decisions.

Simple products often look obvious after they are finished. A clean interface can make a complicated process appear almost effortless.

But simplicity rarely happens by accident. It usually comes from removing things that do not need to be there.

The problem with adding more

When starting a new project, it is tempting to add features immediately. More options seem to create more value.

In practice, every additional feature creates another decision, another interface and another thing that needs maintenance.

Start with the smallest useful version

A useful constraint is to ask one question: what is the smallest version that solves the actual problem?

This approach does not mean making something incomplete. It means being deliberate about what deserves to exist.

Final thoughts

The best version of an idea is not necessarily the version with the most features.

Sometimes the most difficult part of building something is deciding what not to build.
